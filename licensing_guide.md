# Licensing Guide & Open Source Policy

Welcome to the **Vectorial Collective** licensing guide. Because our mission is to make computational physics and engineering simulation tools accessible, transparent, and collaborative, all software repositories under our organization are open-source.

We standardize on the **MIT License** across all our projects. This guide explains what that means, why we use it, and how you can interact with our code.

---

## 1. What is the MIT License?
The MIT License is a short, permissive open-source license. Think of it as a universal permission slip for software. It grants anyone in the world the right to use, study, modify, and share our code, provided they follow a couple of simple rules.

---

## 2. Why We Use It for Vectorial Collective
We chose the MIT License for three core reasons:
* **Maximum Freedom:** We want students, hobbyists, and professional engineers to be able to take our simulation tools, run them, break them, and build amazing things on top of them without bureaucratic red tape.
* **Low Friction for Learning:** Since many of our projects tie into educational paths like Harvard CS50P, we want students to freely experiment, adapt our scripts for their own learning, and showcase their projects.
* **Protection for Creators:** While the license gives everyone freedom to use the code, it also protects our contributors with a strict "no warranty" clause—meaning creators aren't liable if a simulation script behaves unexpectedly.

---

## 3. What You Can and Cannot Do

### ✅ What You CAN Do:
* **Use It:** Run any Vectorial Collective software for personal, academic, or commercial purposes.
* **Modify It:** Change, rewrite, optimize, or build upon our source code.
* **Distribute It:** Share copies of our software or your modified versions with others.
* **Sell It:** You are legally permitted to package and sell software that incorporates our code (though we encourage keeping educational tools free and open!).

### 📋 What You MUST Do:
* **Include the Copyright:** Whenever you distribute our code or a substantial portion of it, you must include the original Vectorial Collective copyright notice and a copy of the MIT License.

### 🚫 What You CANNOT Hold Us Accountable For:
* **No Liability:** The software is provided "as is," without warranty of any kind. If a script fails or gives an incorrect calculation, the original authors and Vectorial Collective cannot be held legally liable or sued.

---

## 4. How to Apply It to Your Contributions
When you contribute code or start a new repository under Vectorial Collective, you don't need to file any paperwork. Simply include a standard `LICENSE` file in the root of your repository containing the MIT text with the current year and organization name. 

If you have questions about how this applies to a specific engineering simulation or collaborative project, feel free to open an issue or reach out to our community!
